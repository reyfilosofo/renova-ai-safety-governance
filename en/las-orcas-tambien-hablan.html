The orcas speak too. We are only just beginning to listen.

Life is woven of signs. Biosemiotics holds that semiosis is coextensive with life, and orcas are one of its most complex voices: acoustic dialects, intergenerational learning, trophic specialization and cooperative hunting. The monograph ℛenova Biosemiotics: Orcas (178 pages) documents the evidence and proposes a research programme on interspecies cooperation. And semiocide — the disruption of natural networks of signification — should enter conservation as a criterion: protecting orcas means protecting a semiosphere, not only a species.
